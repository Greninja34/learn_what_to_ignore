## 1. Title

**Learning What to Ignore: Deriving Invariance for Self-Supervised Text Recognition**

## 2. Abstract (about 120 words)

Text recognisers fail on damaged documents (faded ink, show-through, stains, poor scans), and these are exactly the collections with the fewest transcribed lines. Self-supervised learning (SSL) can learn from unlabelled lines by rebuilding a line from a corrupted copy, but the corruptions it learns to ignore are chosen by hand and re-tuned for every collection. We replace this guess with a measurement: we measure the damage on real historical lines, model each damage type physically, derive how often and how strongly to apply it, and train the recogniser with this derived damage. We test whether it beats hand-picked damage when labels are few, whether the benefit transfers to unseen damage, media and scripts, and whether a model's invariance to a damage predicts how much that damage raises its error.

*Optional last line (pilot):* In the pilot, derived damage used as augmentation lowered the error on real medieval lines from 33.8% to 31.6% CER.

## 3. Research gap (3 points)

1. **SSL for text picks its corruptions by hand.** In SeqCLR, Text-DIAE, LoGo-HTR and others, which damage to use and how strong it is are guessed, then re-tuned for each collection.
2. **Damage fitting exists, but not for deep models.** Fitting degradation models to real scans is old (Kanungo, Haralick, Baird, 2000), but it was built for classic OCR on black-and-white pages. Nobody has used measured real damage to decide **what a deep recogniser should learn to ignore**.
3. **The chain has never been tested:** recipe → what the model ignores (invariance) → error on damaged lines, with damage types, media and scripts held out.

**One line:** *Fitting damage to scans is old; deriving what a recogniser should ignore from real pages, and checking that it does, is new.*

## 4. Objectives (6)

1. A reproducible line-recognition pipeline with strong supervised baselines.
2. A per-line damage-measurement tool, showing how real historical lines differ from clean ones.
3. Physical damage models whose frequency and strength are **derived** from the measurements.
4. Training with the derived damage, compared with no damage, plain masking, hand-picked damage and a VICReg control.
5. Transfer to a held-out damage type, a new medium and an unseen script.
6. The per-damage invariance of every model compared with the rise in error.

*Status:* 1–2 done; 3 done as a first version (v0); 4 partly done; 5–6 first look only, mostly Phase 2–3.
